# State-Specific Advertising Rule Variations

Used by `/marketing-review` once the attorney identifies which state(s) their practice/marketing targets. Apply the ABA baseline (`model-rules-7.1-7.3.md`) plus whichever state section(s) below are configured for this review — a claim can trip the ABA baseline, a state-specific provision, or both.

---

## California

**Governing text:** Cal. Rules of Professional Conduct 7.1–7.5, *plus* California Business & Professions Code §§ 6157–6159.2 — a statutory advertising regime layered on top of the rules, which is unusual among states.

**What to flag beyond the ABA baseline:**
- **Guarantee/warranty language** — Bus. & Prof. Code § 6158.1 separately prohibits statements that guarantee or warrant a specific outcome. Any language implying a guaranteed result ("we guarantee," "you will win," "guaranteed compensation") is a flag under this statute even if it would also be a Rule 7.1 issue.
- **Dramatizations and actors** — statutory provisions require disclosure when an advertisement uses a dramatization, an actor portraying a client or lawyer, or a spokesperson who is not the advertising lawyer, without a clear disclaimer.
- **Unverified "specialist" claims** — claiming to be a "certified specialist" without current certification from an entity accredited by the State Bar of California or the ABA is a specific statutory violation, not just a general 7.1 misrepresentation.

## Texas

**Governing text:** Texas Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct 7.01–7.07 — Texas has its own numbering (7.01–7.07), not the ABA's 7.1–7.5, and the two shouldn't be assumed to line up provision-for-provision.

**What to flag beyond the ABA baseline:**
- **Filing/pre-clearance requirement (Rule 7.07)** — Texas requires many public advertisements and written solicitations to be filed with the Advertising Review Committee of the State Bar of Texas, generally at or before first dissemination, subject to a filing fee — unless the ad qualifies for a narrow exemption (e.g., ads limited to specified basic contact/practice-area information). This is the single biggest Texas-specific gap: flag **⚪ NOT ADDRESSED** and tell the attorney to confirm filing status — this skill has no way to know whether a filing has been made.
- **Prohibited solicitation and payment terms (Rule 7.03)** — Texas has its own specific list of prohibited in-person/telephone solicitation conduct and referral-payment restrictions; treat as a 7.3-equivalent check when the copy is a targeted solicitation.

## New York

**Governing text:** NY Rules of Professional Conduct 7.1–7.5 (22 NYCRR Part 1200).

**What to flag beyond the ABA baseline:**
- **"ATTORNEY ADVERTISING" label** — Rule 7.1(f) requires the words "Attorney Advertising" on the first page (or email subject line) of any advertisement, and on the home page of a website that qualifies as advertising. Flag any reviewed web copy, ad, or email that lacks this label.
- **30-day no-contact rule for accident/disaster victims (Rule 7.3(e))** — a lawyer may not solicit a person, or their relative, who has been involved in a specific accident, disaster, or similar event, or the target of a criminal matter, within 30 days of the incident, where the solicitation relates to that incident. Only relevant to targeted solicitation copy, not general advertising — but flag as **🔴 LIKELY VIOLATION** if the copy is a targeted solicitation referencing a specific recent incident.
- **Filing and retention** — certain advertisements (notably broadcast media) must be filed with the attorney disciplinary committee within a set window of first use, and copies of advertisements generally must be retained for a period of years. Flag as **⚪ NOT ADDRESSED** — this skill can't confirm filing/retention compliance from copy text alone.

## Florida

**Governing text:** Rules Regulating The Florida Bar 4-7.11–4-7.23 — one of the most detailed and prescriptive attorney-advertising regimes in the country.

**What to flag beyond the ABA baseline:**
- **Deceptive/inherently misleading advertisements (Rule 4-7.13)** — a detailed, specific list, including: statements characterizing the quality of the lawyer's services in a way not susceptible to verification; testimonials or endorsements about a matter that is still pending; manipulated images, video, or actors portraying judges or other officials without clear disclosure; dramatizations without an on-screen/on-page "dramatization, not an actual event or client" disclaimer. Flag copy against this list specifically, not just general 7.1 "misleading" language — Florida's list is more granular than the ABA baseline.
- **Potentially misleading advertisements requiring a disclaimer (Rule 4-7.14)** — statements that aren't outright deceptive but require a qualifying disclaimer to avoid being misleading (e.g., a past-results statement).
- **30-day rule for accident/disaster victims (Rule 4-7.18(b))** — materially the same concept as New York's: no direct written/recorded solicitation of an accident or disaster victim (or their relative) within 30 days of the incident, with narrow exceptions (e.g., wrongful-death actions). Flag targeted solicitation copy referencing a specific recent incident as **🔴 LIKELY VIOLATION**.
- **Filing for evaluation (Rule 4-7.19)** — many advertisements must be filed with The Florida Bar for evaluation before or concurrent with first use, subject to a filing fee, unless a specific exemption applies. Flag as **⚪ NOT ADDRESSED** — filing status can't be confirmed from copy text.

## Illinois

**Governing text:** Illinois Rules of Professional Conduct 7.1–7.5 — Illinois tracks the ABA Model Rules more closely than the other four states here, including the consolidation that folded former Rules 7.4/7.5 content into comments.

**What to flag beyond the ABA baseline:**
- **Specialization claims** — a statement claiming to be a "specialist" or "certified specialist" is only permitted if the certifying organization has been accredited by the ABA or the Illinois State Bar Association, and the certifying organization is named in the communication. Flag any specialization claim that doesn't name its certifying body.
- Illinois has comparatively few additional prescriptive provisions beyond the ABA baseline — for most copy, the ABA-baseline analysis in `model-rules-7.1-7.3.md` will carry most of the review. Don't manufacture an Illinois-specific flag where the ABA baseline already covers the issue; cite Illinois's rule number for precision, not as a materially different standard.

## Using this file

1. Ask the attorney which state(s) their marketing targets or where they're licensed, if not already stated.
2. If a named state isn't one of the five above, say so and apply the ABA baseline only — do not invent that state's specific rule numbers or provisions.
3. If more than one state is configured, run the copy against each state's section independently; the same excerpt can be flagged differently in different states (e.g., missing "Attorney Advertising" label is a New York flag but not, on its own, a Texas or Illinois flag).
