# Intake Triage Brief Reference

Reference for `/lead-intake-brief`. Defines what each of the six brief sections structurally contains, the urgency-flag signal categories, the conflict-flag handling rule, and the `[NEEDS: ...]` placeholder convention. This file contains no legal-merit standards, no conflict-of-interest determinations, and no deadline rules — structure only. Whether a lead is worth taking, whether a conflict exists, and what deadline actually applies are all the attorney's own determinations.

---

## The six sections — structure only, not a legal judgment

### Parties

Everyone the intake material names or describes: the prospective client, any opposing party, and any other individual or entity mentioned (a witness, a co-party, an insurer, an employer). State the role each plays as described — do not infer a role that wasn't stated (e.g., don't assume someone is "at fault" because they're named as the other driver).

### Dates

Every date the intake material actually states: when the underlying incident or dispute happened, when the prospective client first reached out, and any other date mentioned — an upcoming hearing, a deadline the lead brought up themselves, a date something was signed or filed. List dates as given; do not calculate a date (e.g., "90 days from the incident") that wasn't stated directly.

### Matter-Type Suggestion

A short suggested label for what kind of matter this looks like (e.g., "possible personal injury — motor vehicle accident," "possible landlord-tenant dispute"), based only on how the intake material describes the situation. Always phrase it as a suggestion for the attorney to confirm ("looks like it may be..."), never as a determination ("this is a viable personal injury claim"). If the intake material doesn't clearly point to one matter type, say so and offer the closest one or two candidates rather than forcing a single label.

### Urgency Flag

A short flag — a level (High / Medium / Low) or a one-line reason — based only on urgency signals actually present in the intake material:

- An explicit deadline or date the lead or the intake material states directly (a hearing date, a response due date, a stated "we need to act by X").
- The lead's own expressed urgency (e.g., "they said it's time-sensitive," "eviction notice already served").
- A mentioned upcoming event with legal consequence (a court date, an expiring insurance claim window) — only if the intake material states it, not inferred from the type of matter.

**Never** derive urgency from a statute of limitations, filing deadline, or other legally-calculated date the skill works out itself from an incident date or matter type. If urgency plausibly depends on a deadline nobody stated, flag it as `[NEEDS: confirm applicable deadline — attorney/docketing system]` rather than estimating one.

### Open Questions

What's missing or unclear that the attorney would want to ask on the consult call — gaps in the facts, contradictions in the intake material, or anything that needs clarifying before the matter-type suggestion or urgency flag can be firmed up. This is also where a possible-conflict flag belongs: if a named party might match an existing client or a name the firm would recognize, note it here as something to check (see below) — never resolve it.

### Recommended Next Step

An operational suggestion — schedule a consult, request a specific document before the call, flag as likely outside the firm's practice areas, or similar — phrased as a suggestion only. Never phrase this as a decision already made ("declined," "accepted," "retained," "we're taking this case").

---

## Conflict-flag handling

This skill never performs or claims to perform a conflict-of-interest check — that determination is the firm's own process, not something this skill runs. This holds even if a client list happens to be present among the attached files: if a named party in the intake material seems like it could match an existing client or a name the firm would recognize, note it in Open Questions as a prompt to check manually (e.g., "opposing party 'Riverside Holdings LLC' — worth a conflict check before the consult"). Never state that a conflict exists or doesn't exist, and never imply the check has already been done.

---

## The `[NEEDS: ...]` placeholder convention

Use `[NEEDS: short description of what's missing]` anywhere a section needs a fact the intake material didn't supply. Examples:

- `[NEEDS: date of the incident]`
- `[NEEDS: opposing party's full name]`
- `[NEEDS: confirm applicable deadline — attorney/docketing system]`

A placeholder is the correct, complete output for that gap — draft the rest of the brief around it rather than pausing the whole draft to ask about every missing detail one at a time. Ask only when a placeholder would leave the brief unusable as a starting point (e.g., no intake material was supplied at all).
