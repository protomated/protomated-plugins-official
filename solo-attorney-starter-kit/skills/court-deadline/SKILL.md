# /court-deadline — Court Deadline Reasoning

> ⚠️ AI-ASSISTED DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED

Surface upcoming court deadlines and filing windows by reading your matter files and calendar, applying jurisdiction-specific deadline reasoning. Designed to catch what a busy solo attorney might miss between matters.

**This skill reads files and calendar events. It does not file anything or create calendar events without your confirmation.**

## Invocation

```
/court-deadline [matter folder path or case name]
```

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Locate the matter

Use the Filesystem connector to find the matter folder. Look for:
- `matter-profile.md`, `intake-summary.md`, or any intake file — parties, case type, jurisdiction, court
- Filed documents, orders, or correspondence naming deadlines
- Any `deadlines.md` or `calendar.md` file the attorney maintains
- Summons, complaint, or initial filing (look for file dates and service dates)

If no matter folder is found, ask the attorney for:
1. Jurisdiction (state/federal court, which district or county)
2. Case type (civil, criminal, family, probate, immigration, etc.)
3. Key triggering dates (filing date, service date, last court order date)

### Step 2 — Scan the calendar

Use the Google Calendar connector to find:
- Any existing events for this matter (search by case name, case number, or client name)
- Hearings, deposition dates, or court appearances already scheduled
- Filing deadlines the attorney has already calendared

Note any gaps between what the files show as pending and what is calendared.

### Step 3 — Identify triggering events and compute deadlines

For each triggering event found in the matter files:

**Common deadline chains to reason through:**

| Trigger | Deadline to compute |
|---|---|
| Complaint served on defendant | Answer/response deadline (21 days federal FRCP; varies by state) |
| Discovery cutoff | Expert disclosures, deposition completion, motions cutoff |
| Answer filed | Discovery opens; initial disclosures (FRCP 14 days from Rule 26(f) conference) |
| Motion filed | Opposition deadline (typically 14–21 days); reply deadline |
| Order entered | Compliance deadline; appeal window (federal: 30 days for civil; 14 days criminal) |
| Statute of limitations | Flag exact expiration date with the source statute |
| Notice of appeal | Record designation, opening brief deadline |

**State-specific notes:**
- State court deadlines vary significantly. Flag jurisdiction and note that the attorney must verify against local rules.
- Federal rules apply in federal court; confirm the district's local rules for any variance.
- Holiday and weekend roll-forward rules: if a deadline falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or federal holiday, it typically rolls to the next business day (FRCP Rule 6(a)(1)(C) for federal; check state equivalent).

### Step 4 — Flag statute of limitations concerns

Identify any:
- Statute of limitations that is within 90 days
- Tolling arguments that may affect the deadline
- Cross-jurisdictional conflicts (e.g., where the injury occurred vs. where filed)

**Always flag SOL concerns prominently. Never downplay a limitations risk.**

## What This Skill Does Not Do

- It does not provide legal advice on whether a deadline applies to your facts.
- It does not access PACER, court dockets, or any external legal database — it works from your local files only.
- It does not calculate deadlines for jurisdictions whose local rules are not in your matter files — it will flag the gap and ask you to provide the rule.
- It does not create calendar events or write files without your explicit confirmation.
