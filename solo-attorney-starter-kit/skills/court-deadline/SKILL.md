Compute a court or filing deadline by applying a rule you supply to a trigger date. Shows every step of the reasoning so you can verify it. Drafts a Google Calendar event — you confirm before it is created.

**HARD NOTE:** This skill does not maintain a database of court rules, local rules, or jurisdiction-specific holiday calendars. You supply the rule. You verify the output. Missed deadlines are the leading cause of legal malpractice claims — always cross-check with your docketing software or certified deadline-calculation service before relying on any computed date.

## Invocation

```
/court-deadline [trigger date] [rule in plain English]
/court-deadline "Complaint served May 12" "responsive pleading due 21 days after service, excluding weekends and federal holidays"
```

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Confirm the inputs

Ask the attorney to confirm or provide:

1. **Trigger date** — the date the clock starts (e.g., date of service, filing date, incident date).
2. **The rule** — in plain English, exactly as the attorney knows it (e.g., "21 calendar days after service," "30 days excluding weekends and state holidays," "within 2 years of the date of injury").
3. **Holiday calendar** — which holidays to exclude, if any (federal holidays, state holidays, court-specific closures). If not specified, default to US federal holidays and note that assumption explicitly.
4. **Event name** — what to call the calendar event (e.g., "DEADLINE: Answer or Responsive Pleading — Smith v. Jones").
5. **Matter reference** — case name or matter folder, for the calendar event description.

Do not proceed until you have the trigger date and the rule. If either is missing, ask before computing.

### Step 2 — Compute the deadline with visible reasoning

Work through the calculation step by step. Show every step:

```
Trigger date:       [date]
Rule:               [exactly as provided by attorney]
Holiday calendar:   [federal / state / other — as specified or defaulted]

Step 1: Add [N] calendar days to [trigger date]
        → Raw deadline: [date]

Step 2: Check whether [raw deadline] falls on a weekend or excluded holiday
        → [date] is a [weekday/weekend/holiday]
        → [If adjustment needed]: Next business day = [adjusted date]

Final computed deadline: [date, day of week]

⚠️ Assumptions made:
- [List any assumption not explicitly stated by attorney, e.g., "Used US federal holiday calendar; confirm whether local court observes additional holidays."]
- [Flag any ambiguity in the rule, e.g., "Rule says 'days' — interpreted as calendar days, not business days. Confirm this is correct."]
```

If the rule is ambiguous (e.g., "days" vs. "business days," or a multi-step calculation), surface the ambiguity and ask the attorney to clarify before finalizing.

### Step 3 — Flag the critical malpractice risk

After presenting the computed date, always add:

> ⚠️ **Verify before docketing:** This date was computed from the rule you provided. It has not been verified against the official court rules, local rules, or standing orders for [court/jurisdiction if known]. Confirm the applicable rule is current and that you have the correct holiday calendar before entering this deadline in your docketing system.

### Step 4 — Draft a calendar event and request confirmation

Present the proposed calendar event details:

```
Event:        [Deadline name]
Date:         [Computed deadline] — ALL DAY
Description:  [Matter name / case number] | Computed from: [trigger date] + [rule] | Computed by: Solo Attorney Claude Starter Kit — VERIFY BEFORE RELYING
Reminder:     7 days before; 1 day before [or ask attorney for preferred lead time]
```

Then ask:

> "Shall I create this calendar event in Google Calendar? Please confirm the date is correct before I proceed. Say 'yes, create it' to add the event, or 'no' to adjust."

Do not create the calendar event until the attorney explicitly confirms.

### Step 5 — Create the calendar event

After explicit confirmation, use the Google Calendar connector to create the event with the details confirmed above.

Confirm completion:

> "Done — deadline event created in Google Calendar: [event name] on [date]. I recommend also entering this date in your docketing software and setting a secondary reminder."
