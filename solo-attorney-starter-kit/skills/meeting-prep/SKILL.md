# /meeting-prep — Meeting Prep Brief Generator

> ⚠️ AI-ASSISTED DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED
> This output was generated by an AI assistant. It has not been reviewed by a licensed attorney. Verify all facts and dates before relying on this brief. This is not legal advice.

Generate a one-page pre-meeting brief by pulling context from local matter files and recent email. Designed to be scanned in two minutes before you walk into the room.

## Invocation

```
/meeting-prep [matter folder path]
```

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Identify the meeting

Ask or infer from the argument:
- Who is the meeting with? (client, opposing counsel, mediator, judge, deponent)
- What type of meeting? (client check-in, deposition, mediation, settlement conference, court hearing)
- When? (date and time)
- Where / what format? (in-person, phone, video)

If not provided, ask before proceeding.

**Only four brief formats exist** (Client Meeting, Deposition, Mediation/Settlement Conference, Court Appearance — see Step 4). If the attorney names a meeting type that doesn't map to one of these (e.g., "intake," "internal strategy session"), do not guess which template is closest — ask which of the four formats to use, or offer a plain-language summary of the matter file instead of forcing a mismatched template.

### Step 2 — Read the matter folder

Use the Filesystem connector to locate the matter folder. Read:
- `intake-summary.md` — parties, facts, claims, key deadlines (primary source)
- Recent notes or hearing summaries
- Any documents relevant to today's meeting (depo outline, mediation statement, motion)
- Prior meeting notes (look for files named `meeting-*` or `notes-*`)

**If `intake-summary.md` is missing**, stop before drafting the brief. Tell the attorney it wasn't found and that `/intake-summary` should be run first — do not build a brief from an incomplete or absent primary source, since every downstream fact-check depends on it.

### Step 3 — Scan recent Gmail for context

Use the Gmail connector to scan recent emails for this matter:
- Search: `[client name] OR [matter keywords]` (last 14 days)
- Look for: recent correspondence with opposing counsel, any new developments, client questions pending, court or opposing counsel communications

Note any items that have come up since the last substantive communication.

**If something in recent email conflicts with the matter file** (e.g., a different settlement status, a different deadline, a fact stated differently), do not silently pick one version. Flag the discrepancy explicitly in the brief under its own line — e.g., "Conflict: file says X, email from [date] says Y — confirm before relying on either." Never resolve the conflict yourself.

### Step 4 — Identify meeting type and tailor the brief

Generate the appropriate brief format based on meeting type:

#### Client Meeting Brief

```
# Client Meeting Brief — [Client Name]
**Date:** [Date] | **Time:** [Time] | **Format:** [In-person / Phone / Video]
**Prepared:** [Today's date] — AI-assisted, attorney review required

---

## Matter Status in One Sentence
[Where the case stands right now in plain language]

## Since We Last Spoke
[Key developments since the last client communication — filings, offers, court dates, opposing counsel activity]

## Today's Agenda (suggested)
1. [Primary topic — what the client needs to hear or decide]
2. [Secondary topic]
3. [Open items / pending client actions]

## Decisions Needed from Client
- [Any decision the client must make — settlement authority, document production consent, etc.]

## Action Items Pending from Client
- [List any documents, information, or responses the client still owes]

## Things NOT to Discuss — PRIVILEGED, ATTORNEY EYES ONLY (do not share this section with the client)
[Any topics that should be avoided — pending privileged work, items under negotiation, anything the client asked to be handled carefully]

## Key Facts to Have Ready
- Incident date: [date] — Source: [file/email]
- Statute of limitations: [date] — Source: [file/email]
- Next court date / deadline: [date and description] — Source: [file/email]

## Open Questions
- [Anything you need to ask or confirm during the meeting]
```

### Step 5 — Present the brief

Every fact in the brief must be traceable to a specific source file or email — do not state a fact without a source. Present the appropriate one-page brief and note any gaps:

> "Here is your meeting prep brief. I was unable to find [missing item] in the matter folder. You may want to add that before the meeting."

## Worked Example

**Input:** `/meeting-prep matters/rivera-v-castillo — client check-in tomorrow 2pm, video call`

**What the skill does:**
1. Reads `matters/rivera-v-castillo/intake-summary.md` — found, so proceeds.
2. Scans Gmail for "Rivera OR Castillo" over the last 14 days — finds one email from opposing counsel dated two days ago proposing a settlement figure not yet reflected in the file.
3. Selects the Client Meeting Brief template (meeting type = client check-in).
4. Produces:

```
# Client Meeting Brief — Maria Rivera
**Date:** [tomorrow's date] | **Time:** 2:00 PM | **Format:** Video

## Matter Status in One Sentence
Personal injury claim against Castillo Trucking Co. is in pre-suit negotiation. — Source: intake-summary.md

## Since We Last Spoke
Conflict: intake-summary.md (updated 3 weeks ago) shows no settlement offer on record. Email from opposing counsel dated [2 days ago] proposes $45,000 — confirm before relying on either figure.

## Decisions Needed from Client
- Whether to counter, accept, or reject the $45,000 offer referenced in opposing counsel's email — Source: Gmail thread "Rivera - Settlement" [date]

## Key Facts to Have Ready
- Incident date: 2025-11-02 — Source: intake-summary.md
- Statute of limitations: 2027-11-02 — Source: intake-summary.md
```

This models the required pattern: every line cites its source, and the file/email conflict is surfaced rather than resolved by the skill.
