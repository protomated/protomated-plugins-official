# /research-memo — Verified-Source Legal Research Memo Drafter

> ⚠️ AI-ASSISTED DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED
> This output was generated by an AI assistant. Every citation must be checked against the original source document before this memo is relied on, filed, or sent. This is not legal advice, and it is not the product of independent legal research — it only reflects the documents you supplied.

Draft a legal research memo that answers your question using **only** the source documents you've attached to a workspace folder. This skill does not search the open web, does not query any case-law or statute database, and does not fall back on the model's general legal knowledge to fill a citation. If your attached sources don't support part of the answer, that part is flagged as unsupported — never quietly filled in.

**This skill only cites what you gave it. It never invents or infers a citation, and it never writes a file without your explicit confirmation.**

## Why this skill exists

Hallucinated citations are not a hypothetical risk — they are actively producing bar discipline and sanctions for practicing attorneys, including a Ninth Circuit six-month suspension of two immigration attorneys and DOJ sanctions threats against government counsel. Generic AI research tools that draw on open-ended training knowledge or unverified web search invite exactly this failure mode. This skill is built the opposite way: it treats "I don't have a source for that" as a valid and required answer, not a gap to paper over.

## Invocation

```
/research-memo [legal question] — [path to source-document folder]
/research-memo "Does the indemnification clause in the Acme MSA cover third-party IP claims?" — ~/Matters/Acme-Contract/sources
/research-memo "Is the traffic stop supported by reasonable suspicion under these facts?" — ~/Matters/Delgado-Auto-2026/sources
```

If no folder path is given, ask for one before proceeding. If the attorney names a matter but not a specific sources subfolder, look for a `sources/` or `research/` folder at the top of that matter, or ask where the source documents live.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Gather the question and the source set

Confirm with the attorney:
1. **The precise legal question** — if it's vague ("research breach of contract"), ask for the specific issue, the governing jurisdiction if relevant, and any facts the analysis should apply to.
2. **The source folder path** — must be inside the connected Filesystem workspace.
3. **Matter context (optional)** — if a matter folder with `intake-summary.md` is identifiable, read it for party names and facts, but do not treat it as a legal-authority source; it informs the Statement of Facts, not the Discussion's citations.

State the rule up front, once, so the attorney knows what's about to happen:

> "I'll answer this using only the documents in [folder path]. I will not search the web or cite anything from general legal knowledge — if the attached documents don't cover part of the question, I'll flag that instead of guessing."

### Step 2 — Index the source documents

Use the Filesystem connector to read every file in the source folder (and subfolders, one level deep). For each file, extract:
- **Identifier** — case name/citation if it's a case, statute/section number if it's a statute, document title otherwise.
- **What it is** — case, statute, regulation, brief, contract, deposition excerpt, etc.
- **Page/paragraph structure** — note page numbers or numbered paragraphs so later citations can point to a specific location, not just the filename.

Build a source index and show it before drafting:

```
Source index — [folder path]
1. [filename] — [identifier, e.g. "Smith v. Jones, 214 F.3d 100 (case)"] — [N pages]
2. [filename] — [identifier, e.g. "Cal. Civ. Code § 1542 (statute excerpt)"] — [N pages]
3. [filename] — [⚠️ Could not extract readable text — skipped. If this is a scanned PDF or image, re-save as text-based PDF or .md/.txt]
```

If the folder is empty or contains no legible documents, stop here:

> "The folder [path] has no readable source documents, so I can't draft a grounded memo. Attach the cases, statutes, or briefs you want this memo built on, then run the skill again."

Do not proceed to drafting on an empty or unreadable source set.

### Step 3 — Assess source relevance before drafting

Before writing, check whether the indexed sources actually bear on the question asked. If none of them are relevant to the question, stop and say so rather than drafting a memo padded with tangential citations or unsupported general statements:

> "None of the documents in [folder path] address [the question]. I'd be guessing or relying on general knowledge to answer this, which this skill doesn't do. Attach sources that speak to this specific question, or narrow the question to what these documents actually cover."

If only some elements of the question are covered, proceed to Step 4 but plan to flag the uncovered elements individually rather than declining the whole memo.

### Step 4 — Draft the memo, citing only indexed sources

Structure:

```markdown
# Research Memo: [Question, short form]

**To:** [Attorney name, if known]
**From:** AI-assisted draft — attorney review required
**Date:** [Date]
**Re:** [Matter name, if applicable] — [Legal question, full form]

## Question Presented
[Restate the question precisely, including jurisdiction and key facts if given.]

## Brief Answer
[1–3 sentence direct answer, hedged appropriately if sources only partially support it. If no source-backed answer is possible, say so here rather than in Discussion only.]

## Statement of Facts
[Facts as provided by the attorney or drawn from intake-summary.md / the question itself — not from the legal-authority sources. Flag any fact gap as [FACT NOT PROVIDED — confirm with attorney].]

## Discussion

### [Issue 1]
[Analysis paragraph. Every legal proposition carries an inline citation to an indexed source: (Smith v. Jones, [filename], p. 4). Where the sources don't support a needed step in the reasoning, write: [No supplied source addresses this point — attorney must supply authority or the analysis stops here] rather than continuing with an uncited assertion.]

### [Issue 2, if applicable]
[Same rule.]

## Conclusion
[Summary tied back to the Brief Answer. If material parts of the question went unanswered for lack of sources, restate that limitation plainly — do not let the Conclusion imply more certainty than the Discussion supports.]
```

**Hard citation rule, no exceptions:**
- Every citation in the Discussion must point to a document in the Step 2 source index, with a page or paragraph locator.
- Never cite a case, statute, or rule the model recognizes from training but that is not in the indexed source set — even if it is well-known and almost certainly correct. If it's not in the folder, it does not appear as a citation.
- Never present a claim as settled law without a citation to an indexed source. An unsupported point is written as a flagged gap, not as unattributed prose that reads like a citation was implied.

### Step 5 — Append the mandatory verification checklist

Every memo ends with a checklist listing every citation used, so the attorney can verify each one against the original document before relying on the memo:

```markdown
## Citation Verification Checklist

**Before using this memo, confirm each citation against its source document:**

| # | Citation as used in memo | Source file | Page/¶ | Verified? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Smith v. Jones, 214 F.3d 100 | smith-v-jones.pdf | p. 4 | ☐ |
| 2 | Cal. Civ. Code § 1542 | ccc-1542-excerpt.pdf | p. 1 | ☐ |

**Unsupported points flagged in this memo (no citation provided — do not treat as researched):**
- [List each flagged gap from the Discussion, or "None" if every point was source-backed.]

This memo reflects only the documents in [folder path] as of [date]. It is not comprehensive legal research — it does not search case law, statutes, or secondary sources beyond what you attached.
```

### Step 6 — Present the draft and confirm before saving

Show the full memo (including the checklist) and ask:

> "Here is the draft memo, built only from the documents in [folder path]. Please review the citations against the checklist above before relying on this. Would you like me to save this to [matter folder]/research/[topic]-memo.md? Say 'yes, save it' to proceed, or tell me what to change."

Do not write any file without explicit confirmation. If the attorney requests changes, revise and re-present — do not introduce a new citation at this stage unless the attorney points to a new source document to add to the index (repeat Step 2 for it first).

## What This Skill Does Not Do

- It does not search the open web, Google Scholar, Westlaw, Lexis, CourtListener, or any case-law or statute database. It only reads what you've attached.
- It does not cite anything from the model's general legal knowledge, even settled or well-known law — if it's not in your source folder, it's not cited.
- It does not verify that your source documents are still good law, current, or accurate — that verification is on you, which is exactly what the checklist is for.
- It does not perform comprehensive legal research or tell you what sources you're missing beyond flagging where your current set doesn't answer the question.
- It does not file, send, or submit the memo anywhere — it drafts, you review, you decide.
