# /intake-summary — Intake Summary Processor

> ⚠️ AI-ASSISTED DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED
> This output was generated by an AI assistant. It has not been reviewed by a licensed attorney. Review all facts, dates, and claims before relying on this summary or sharing it with anyone. This is not legal advice.

Convert raw intake notes into a structured case brief. The output becomes the `intake-summary.md` file at the top of the matter folder — the anchor document every other skill reads from.

## Invocation

```
/intake-summary [matter folder path]
/intake-summary [paste raw notes here]
```

---

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Collect raw intake data

**Option A — From a matter folder:**
Use the Filesystem connector to locate the matter folder. Look for:
- Any `.md`, `.txt`, or `.docx` intake file
- Raw notes from a consultation
- A completed intake form (PDF or text)

**Option B — From pasted notes:**
If the attorney pastes notes directly, accept them as-is and proceed.

**If neither is available**, ask the attorney to provide or paste the intake information. Prompt with the fields below.

### Step 2 — Extract and structure the information

Identify and organize the following fields from the raw notes. Mark any field as `[Not provided]` if absent — do not infer or fabricate.

**Parties:**
- Client full name, date of birth (if relevant), address, phone, email
- Counterparty / adverse party name, address, known contact info
- Relevant third parties (insurers, employers, witnesses)

**Matter:**
- Practice area / claim type (personal injury, contract dispute, family law, employment, etc.)
- Brief factual summary: what happened, when, where, who was involved
- Date of incident or triggering event
- Prior legal action (existing case number, prior attorneys, settlements)

**Damages or Relief Sought:**
- Claimed injuries, losses, or damages (itemize if notes permit)
- Type of relief requested (monetary, injunctive, declaratory)

**Deadlines and Statutes of Limitations:**
- Applicable statute of limitations (flag if attorney has not addressed this — it is a critical malpractice risk)
- Any known filing deadlines, response deadlines, or hearing dates
- Contract deadlines or notice requirements

**Evidence and Documents:**
- Documents client provided or promised to provide
- Known evidence (photos, contracts, medical records, pay stubs)
- Witnesses identified

**Conflicts:**
- Any adverse parties, witnesses, or related entities that may trigger a conflicts check
- Note: the attorney must run a conflicts check before opening the file

**Fee Agreement:**
- Fee type discussed (hourly / flat / contingency)
- Any amounts or rates mentioned
- Retainer deposit discussed

**Next Steps:**
- Actions agreed to in the consultation
- Items client needs to provide
- Attorney's planned next actions and timeline

### Step 3 — Flag critical items

After structuring the summary, add a "Critical Flags" section for:
- Statute of limitations: note the deadline or flag that it must be verified immediately if not confirmed in the notes.
- Conflicts: flag any potential conflicts of interest identified in the notes.
- Missing documents: list any documents mentioned but not yet received.
- Anything that suggests the matter may be outside the attorney's expertise or capacity.

### Step 5 — Present summary and ask to save

Show the full summary and ask:

> "Would you like me to save this as `intake-summary.md` in [matter folder path]? Once saved, the other skills (engagement-letter, meeting-prep, new-matter-organizer) will use this file as the source of record for this matter."

Do not write the file without explicit confirmation.

## What This Skill Does Not Do

- It does not evaluate the merits of the claim.
- It does not calculate or confirm the statute of limitations — it flags the issue for attorney verification.
- It does not run a conflicts check — it identifies names the attorney needs to check.
- It does not open the matter file or take any case action.
