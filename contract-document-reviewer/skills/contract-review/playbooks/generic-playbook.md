> GENERIC PLAYBOOK — NOT FIRM-SPECIFIC. This is a conservative, general-purpose starting point for common commercial contract clause types (e.g., NDAs, MSAs, vendor and service agreements). It does not encode this firm's actual negotiation positions, industry, deal size, or risk tolerance. Use it as a first pass only — replace it with your firm's own `playbook.md`, or have a licensed attorney independently verify every rating and redline, before using this review in negotiation. # Generic Commercial Contract Playbook

Format per [`reference/review-rubric.md`](../reference/review-rubric.md): Clause Type, Firm Position (🟢), Acceptable Fallback (🟡), Red Flags (🔴), Suggested Redline Language.

---

## Limitation of Liability

**🟢 Firm position:** Liability is capped at fees paid (or payable) in the 12 months preceding the claim, with mutual carve-outs for confidentiality breach, IP infringement, and gross negligence/willful misconduct.

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A higher cap (e.g., 24 months of fees, or a fixed dollar amount) if the deal size warrants it, or a cap that's one-directional if the client is clearly the lower-risk party in the relationship.

**🔴 Red flags:** No cap at all; a cap that applies only to the counterparty's liability and not the client's; consequential/indirect damages not excluded on both sides.

**Suggested redline language:** "Each party's total liability arising out of or related to this Agreement shall not exceed the fees paid or payable under this Agreement in the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim, except for breaches of confidentiality, infringement of intellectual property rights, and claims arising from gross negligence or willful misconduct."

---

## Indemnification

**🟢 Firm position:** Mutual indemnification, scoped to third-party claims arising from breach of the agreement, IP infringement, or a party's gross negligence/willful misconduct — not a broad, open-ended obligation.

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** One-directional indemnification if the client is the party providing a product/service and the scope is limited to IP infringement and confidentiality breach specifically.

**🔴 Red flags:** Uncapped or unlimited indemnification obligation on the client; indemnification scoped to "any and all claims" with no limitation to third-party claims or specific triggers; no corresponding limitation-of-liability cap applying to the indemnity.

**Suggested redline language:** "Each party shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the other party from third-party claims arising from (a) the indemnifying party's breach of this Agreement, (b) infringement of intellectual property rights, or (c) the indemnifying party's gross negligence or willful misconduct, subject to the limitation of liability set forth in Section [__]."

---

## Termination

**🟢 Firm position:** Either party may terminate for material breach with a cure period (typically 30 days' written notice), and either party may terminate for convenience with reasonable advance notice (typically 30–90 days depending on deal length).

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A longer cure period or notice period than the firm's baseline, or a termination-for-convenience right that requires a minimum committed term before it becomes available.

**🔴 Red flags:** Only the counterparty has a termination-for-convenience right; no cure period for material breach; automatic renewal with no reasonable opt-out notice window (e.g., less than 30 days).

**Suggested redline language:** "Either party may terminate this Agreement (a) for material breach by the other party that remains uncured thirty (30) days after written notice, or (b) for convenience upon thirty (30) days' prior written notice to the other party."

---

## Confidentiality / Non-Disclosure

**🟢 Firm position:** Mutual confidentiality obligation, standard carve-outs (information that's public, independently developed, rightfully received from a third party, or required to be disclosed by law), and a defined survival period after termination (typically 2–5 years, or indefinite for trade secrets).

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A shorter survival period if the deal is low-sensitivity, or one-directional confidentiality if only one party is genuinely disclosing sensitive information.

**🔴 Red flags:** No carve-outs at all; confidentiality obligation with no time limit and no trade-secret distinction (an indefinite blanket term is a red flag unless expressly limited to trade secrets); one-directional obligation that binds only the client when both parties are exchanging sensitive information.

**Suggested redline language:** "Each party's confidentiality obligations under this Agreement shall survive for five (5) years following termination or expiration of this Agreement, except that obligations with respect to trade secrets shall survive for as long as the information remains a trade secret under applicable law."

---

## Governing Law & Dispute Resolution

**🟢 Firm position:** Governing law and venue in the client's home state, or a neutral state both parties agree to; dispute resolution that doesn't foreclose the client's practical ability to bring a claim (e.g., no mandatory arbitration in a distant forum with prohibitive costs).

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** Counterparty's home state if the deal size and relationship don't justify pushing back, or arbitration if the venue and cost-allocation terms are reasonable.

**🔴 Red flags:** Mandatory arbitration in a forum that's prohibitively expensive or distant relative to the deal size, with no cost-shifting provision; a jury-trial or class-action waiver bundled in without the client's attention being drawn to it; venue in a jurisdiction with no rational connection to either party.

**Suggested redline language:** "This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of the State of [__], without regard to conflict-of-laws principles, and any dispute shall be resolved in the state or federal courts located in [__]."

---

## Payment Terms

**🟢 Firm position:** Clear payment schedule and due dates, a defined invoice-dispute process, and a reasonable late-payment remedy (e.g., interest at a stated rate, not an automatic default/termination trigger).

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** Shorter payment terms (e.g., net 15 instead of net 30) if that's standard for the deal type, or a modest early-termination fee tied to unrecovered costs.

**🔴 Red flags:** No defined due date or invoice-dispute mechanism; a late payment automatically triggering default, acceleration, or termination with no cure period; payment terms that are one-sided with no reciprocal remedy if the counterparty is late paying the client.

**Suggested redline language:** "Payment is due within thirty (30) days of the invoice date. Any good-faith dispute over an invoiced amount must be raised in writing within fifteen (15) days of receipt; undisputed amounts remain due on the original schedule."

---

## Intellectual Property Assignment / License

**🟢 Firm position:** Ownership of pre-existing IP stays with the party that created it; only IP specifically developed for and paid for under this agreement is assigned or licensed, and the scope of any license granted is limited to what's needed to perform the agreement.

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A broader license grant (rather than full assignment) if that meets the counterparty's actual need, or a carve-back license letting the client continue using background IP incorporated into a deliverable.

**🔴 Red flags:** Blanket assignment of all IP the client creates during the contract term, regardless of relation to the engagement; no carve-out for the client's pre-existing tools, templates, or background IP; a license grant broader than needed to use the deliverable (e.g., unlimited sublicensing rights with no restriction).

**Suggested redline language:** "Each party retains ownership of its pre-existing intellectual property. Intellectual property specifically developed for and paid for under this Agreement is assigned to [Client] upon full payment; each party grants the other a non-exclusive, limited license to use any pre-existing IP solely as necessary to perform or receive the benefit of this Agreement."

---

## Warranties & Disclaimers

**🟢 Firm position:** Reasonable, specific warranties tied to what the party can actually control (e.g., authority to enter the agreement, non-infringement, compliance with applicable law) — not a blanket "AS IS" disclaimer that guts every warranty a client would reasonably expect.

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A disclaimer of implied warranties (merchantability, fitness for a particular purpose) paired with express warranties that remain intact, which is standard commercial practice.

**🔴 Red flags:** A disclaimer that eliminates express warranties along with implied ones; no warranty of authority to enter the agreement or of non-infringement; warranties that run only in the counterparty's favor with none reciprocal to the client.

**Suggested redline language:** "Each party represents and warrants that it has the full right and authority to enter into this Agreement, and that its performance will not infringe the intellectual property rights of any third party. EXCEPT AS EXPRESSLY SET FORTH IN THIS AGREEMENT, EACH PARTY DISCLAIMS ALL IMPLIED WARRANTIES, INCLUDING MERCHANTABILITY AND FITNESS FOR A PARTICULAR PURPOSE."

---

## Non-Compete / Non-Solicitation

**🟢 Firm position:** Narrowly scoped in duration (typically 12 months or less), geography, and subject matter, and limited to what's reasonably necessary to protect a legitimate business interest; note that enforceability of any non-compete is state-law-dependent and some states restrict or void them entirely — flag rather than rate silently.

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A longer duration or broader geography if tied to a genuinely broad, ongoing relationship, provided the scope still targets a specific, legitimate interest rather than blanket market exclusion.

**🔴 Red flags:** Indefinite or unreasonably long duration; geographic or subject-matter scope broader than the actual relationship justifies; a non-solicitation clause that reaches employees or customers the client never had contact with under this agreement.

**Suggested redline language:** "For a period of twelve (12) months following termination of this Agreement, [Party] shall not [specific restricted activity], limited to [specific geography/subject matter]. This provision is intended to be interpreted consistently with applicable state law governing restrictive covenants, and any state execution or enforceability requirement is outside the scope of this review — attorney to confirm independently."

---

## Force Majeure

**🟢 Firm position:** Mutual force-majeure clause covering events genuinely outside either party's control (natural disaster, war, government order, widespread infrastructure failure), with a notice requirement and a right to terminate if the event persists beyond a defined period.

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A shorter or longer persistence-before-termination window depending on deal criticality, or a narrower list of qualifying events if that's standard for the transaction type.

**🔴 Red flags:** One-directional force majeure that excuses only the counterparty's performance; a definition broad enough to excuse ordinary business risk (e.g., "any event affecting a party's ability to perform," with no genuine-unforeseeability requirement); no notice obligation or termination right if the event is prolonged.

**Suggested redline language:** "Neither party shall be liable for delay or failure to perform due to causes beyond its reasonable control, including natural disaster, war, or governmental order, provided the affected party gives prompt written notice. If such event continues for more than sixty (60) days, either party may terminate this Agreement upon written notice."

---

## Assignment

**🟢 Firm position:** Neither party may assign the agreement without the other's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), with a standard carve-out allowing assignment in connection with a merger, acquisition, or sale of substantially all assets.

**🟡 Acceptable fallback:** A consent requirement that's more restrictive for one party than the other, if that reflects a genuine difference in the parties' bargaining position and deal structure.

**🔴 Red flags:** Free assignability by the counterparty with no consent requirement at all, while the client remains restricted; no carve-out for a corporate transaction, effectively trapping the client in a relationship with an unknown successor entity with no exit right.

**Suggested redline language:** "Neither party may assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other party, not to be unreasonably withheld, except that either party may assign this Agreement without consent in connection with a merger, acquisition, or sale of substantially all of its assets, upon written notice to the other party."
