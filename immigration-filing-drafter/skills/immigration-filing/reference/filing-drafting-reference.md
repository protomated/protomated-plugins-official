# Filing & Status-Update Drafting Reference

Reference for `/immigration-filing`. Defines what each narrative type structurally contains, the status-update trigger categories, and the placeholder convention used when a fact, argument, or deadline wasn't supplied. This file contains no immigration-law claims, no case-outcome guidance, and no deadline rules — narrative structure only. Legal argument, current filing requirements, and deadlines come only from the firm's own template and what the attorney supplies for that specific case.

---

## Narrative types — structure only, not legal content

### Support letter

A letter accompanying a petition or application that summarizes the case and points to the evidence supporting it. Typically covers: who is filing and on whose behalf, what's being requested, a plain summary of the relevant facts (relationship, employment, eligibility basis — whatever the filing type calls for), and a list of what evidence is enclosed and what each piece shows. The persuasive legal argument for *why* the facts satisfy the legal standard is the attorney's own content — organize the facts and evidence into the structure the firm's template calls for; leave `[NEEDS: attorney's legal argument/citation]` for anything beyond that.

### Cover letter

The transmittal letter listing what's enclosed in a filing package — a checklist function, not a persuasive one. Typically covers: the filing type and receipt/case number if one exists, the sender and recipient, and an itemized list of enclosed forms and evidence. Keep this factual and list-based; it does not need or want persuasive narrative content.

### RFE-response outline

An outline organizing the firm's response to a Request for Evidence, addressing each point USCIS raised. Structure it point-by-point: what USCIS asked for, what evidence the firm is submitting in response, and (if the template calls for it) a short factual note on how that evidence addresses the request. The legal argument for why the submitted evidence satisfies the RFE is the attorney's own content — leave `[NEEDS: attorney's legal argument/citation]` for anything beyond restating the request and listing the responsive evidence.

## Status-update email — trigger categories

These are prompts for what "status change" usually means, not legal categories to force onto every email. Draft only from what the attorney actually reported for this specific matter — do not assume a category's typical next step applies unless the attorney stated it.

- **RFE issued** — USCIS requested additional evidence. Report what was requested (as described by the attorney) and any next step the attorney gave; do not characterize how likely the RFE is to be resolved favorably.
- **RFE response filed** — the firm submitted its response. Report that it was filed; do not state or imply an outcome.
- **Biometrics scheduled / completed** — an appointment date or completion, as reported.
- **Interview scheduled** — a date and location, as reported, plus any attorney-specified preparation instructions.
- **Additional evidence requested informally** (not a formal RFE) — treat the same as an RFE for drafting purposes: report the request, no outcome characterization.
- **Approved** — report the approval and any next step the attorney specifies (e.g., "your card will arrive by mail"). Do not add detail about typical processing times beyond what the attorney stated.
- **Denied** — report the denial and any next step the attorney specifies. This is a sensitive email; keep it factual and let the attorney's own stated next steps carry the guidance — do not add legal characterization of the denial or its reasons.
- **Transferred** (e.g., to another service center) — report the transfer and any attorney-specified implication (e.g., expected processing-time change), only if the attorney stated it.
- **Other** — any attorney-reported change not listed above; draft the same way — factual, from what was reported, no added prediction or legal characterization.

## The `[NEEDS: ...]` placeholder convention

Use `[NEEDS: short description of what's missing]` anywhere a narrative section or status email needs a fact, a piece of legal argument/citation, or a deadline that wasn't supplied. Examples:

- `[NEEDS: exact date of marriage]`
- `[NEEDS: A-number]`
- `[NEEDS: attorney's legal argument connecting this evidence to the eligibility requirement]`
- `[NEEDS: response deadline — confirm with your docketing system]`

A placeholder is the correct, complete output for that gap — draft the rest of the section around it rather than pausing the whole draft to ask about every missing detail one at a time. Ask only when a placeholder would leave the draft unusable as a starting point (e.g., the narrative type itself, or which matter this is for).
